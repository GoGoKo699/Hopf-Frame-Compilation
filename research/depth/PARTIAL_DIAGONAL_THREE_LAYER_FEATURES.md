# Three native layers with a fixed number of non-diagonal coordinates

[Three-layer native structure](THREE_LAYER_NATIVE_STRUCTURE.md) ·
[Pauli-boundary degree theorem](PAULI_BOUNDARY_THREE_LAYER_RIGIDITY.md) ·
[Occupied logical transport](OCCUPIED_LOGICAL_TRANSPORT.md)

This theorem concerns an actual full-space unitary. It does not infer a
full-space Clifford identity from agreement on an initialized sector.
The input assumption is that a zero-clean native three-band unitary
preserves a computational-Z subgroup of fixed codimension. The remaining
quantum blocks then depend on a bounded number of low-degree Boolean
features, independently of the total width and number of middle pairs.

## 1. Statement and explicit constants

Let V be a Clifford+T unitary on Q arbitrary qubits, with at most three
native T bands. Let M be a computational-Z Pauli subgroup of dimension
$`Q-k`$, and suppose

```math
VPV^\dagger=P\qquad(P\in M).                       \qquad(1)
```

Choose fixed invertible affine computational coordinates in which M is
generated by the Z operators of a central register z. The other k qubits
form a quantum register, so

```math
V=\sum_z|z\rangle\langle z|\otimes V(z).
```

Put

```math
\begin{gathered}
q_k=2k+4,\qquad N_k=2^{2k^2+5k+10},\\
B_k=9\,2^k+2k+3,\\
D_k=48q_kN_k+4B_k+k.                              \qquad(2)
\end{gathered}
```

**Finite-feature theorem.** There are at most B_k Boolean features of z
of degree at most four and at most $`3N_k`$ features of degree at most
$`16q_k`$ that together determine the entire exact matrix V(z), up to
one fixed retained common scalar. The features and the resulting finite
matrix-valued function may depend on the actual circuit; their number
and degree bounds depend only on k.

Consequently, if on a fixed computational core sector, or on all core
inputs, V has a literal or projective Boolean diagonal action, its sign
polynomial has degree at most D_k. A fixed sector specifies some core
bits and leaves the others arbitrary; count the remaining at-most-k
core input bits as variables.
The statement includes a fixed initialized core sector whenever (1) is
already a full-space property of the supplied circuit. It does not derive
(1) from that initialization promise.

The constants are deliberately generous. For one quantum coordinate,

```math
D_1=37748829\lt2^{26}.                            \qquad(3)
```

The same bound with one additional degree-one feature allows multiplication
by a fixed Pauli T or T-dagger rotation commuting with M. Its core Pauli
label is fixed and its sign is one affine central parity.

## 2. The native pair reduction only needs the preserved subgroup

Absorb intermediate Cliffords into actual independent commuting axis
lists and write

```math
V=K_C^\dagger E_C^\dagger E_BE_AK_A.                \qquad(4)
```

For each P in M, the full-space preservation identity gives

```math
J_P=E_AK_APK_A^\dagger E_A^\dagger,\qquad
E_BJ_PE_B^\dagger=E_CK_CPK_C^\dagger E_C^\dagger.   \qquad(5)
```

Both sides of the middle passage are actual Hermitian Cliffords. Native
axis matching therefore applies. The A-band displacement maps have the
form

```math
N_Pv=\sum_i\lambda_i(P)\langle a_i,v\rangle a_i,
\qquad N_PN_{P'}=0.                               \qquad(6)
```

The exact orbit, signed-character and independent rank-one-tensor
arguments from the zero-clean three-layer theorem use only (5), (6)
and physical axis independence. They do not require M to be maximal.
Thus middle orbits are singletons or pairs. For each pair its sign
character is zero or its swap character, permitting the same exact
orientation adjustment as in that theorem. The corresponding whole
pair phase then commutes every J_P literally.

Peel the globally silent A axes to the input. Their pullback is a native
phase D_A commuting M. Peel each oriented middle pair to the input side
of the remaining source. Denote its pullback by F_j. Every F_j commutes
M, all F_j commute with one another, and

```math
F_j^8=I.                                         \qquad(7)
```

These facts hold on the entire physical space. A pair factor is the
conjugate of two commuting literal T/T-dagger phases, which proves (7)
without a claim that the factor is Clifford.

For a pair with Hermitian axes B,D put R=BD and

```math
\mathcal R=K_A^\dagger R K_A,\qquad
H=K_A^\dagger E_A^\dagger B E_AK_A.                \qquad(8)
```

Here E_A denotes the active A part after the silent factor was peeled.
The pair difference lies in the A span, so E_A commutes R. The signed
pair action fixes R literally. Equation (5) then shows that
$`\mathcal R`$ commutes M. H is a Hermitian Clifford commuting
$`\mathcal R`$. The conjugate of D is $`H\mathcal R`$.
With $`\Pi_\pm=(I\pm\mathcal R)/2`$ and
$`S_H=((1+i)I+(1-i)H)/2`$, the two possible literal factors are

```math
F_j=\Pi_+S_H+\omega\Pi_-,\qquad
F_j=\Pi_++\omega^{-1}\Pi_-S_H,
\qquad\omega=e^{i\pi/4}.                          \qquad(9)
```

Thus H occurs only on one specified Pauli eigensector. The other sector
is a known scalar. The Pauli $`\mathcal R`$ can act nontrivially on the
k quantum coordinates; it is not assumed to be a central parity.

## 3. A literal Clifford extension for each active Pauli sector

Let the active sector in (9) be $`\mathcal R=s`$, with
$`s\in\{1,-1\}`$. Because F_j commutes M and the coefficient of H
on that sector is nonzero, H has the same action as identity by
conjugation on every P in M when restricted to that sector. Since H
is an actual Clifford, comparison of the two Pauli images gives

```math
HPH^\dagger=P(s\mathcal R)^{e(P)},\qquad
e:M\longrightarrow\mathbb F_2\text{ linear}.       \qquad(10)
```

Indeed, two Paulis agreeing on a half-dimensional Pauli eigenspace differ
by identity or its signed stabilizer. An anticommuting Pauli product
has eigenvalues $`\pm i`$ and cannot fix that eigenspace. Multiplying
the actual conjugation identities proves the character law in (10).

Choose a Hermitian Pauli Q_0 commuting $`\mathcal R`$ with
$`\langle Q_0,P\rangle=e(P)`$ for every P in M. This is possible
by symplectic duality: if $`\mathcal R\in M`$, the compatibility
condition $`e(\mathcal R)=0`$ follows from H commuting
$`\mathcal R`$. Define the actual controlled Pauli

```math
U=\Pi_{\mathcal R=s}I+\Pi_{\mathcal R=-s}Q_0,
\qquad H'=UH.                                    \qquad(11)
```

U is Clifford, fixes the active sector pointwise and commutes
$`\mathcal R`$. Its Pauli conjugation is the correction in (10),
so H' fixes every P in M pointwise. It also commutes
$`\mathcal R`$ and agrees with H on the active sector. In (9), H can
therefore be replaced by H' with no change to F_j.

H' need not be Hermitian on the inactive sector. Neither the proof nor
the formula uses that sector of H'. This is a Clifford extension from
an actual Pauli code with the correction (11) supplied explicitly. It
is not an extension from a non-Pauli clean code.

In the central coordinates of Section 1, a Clifford fixing M has blocks

```math
H'(z)=\gamma i^{a(z)}X^{u(z)}Z^{v(z)}H_*,\qquad
\mathcal R(z)=(-1)^{\ell(z)}R_* .                 \qquad(12)
```

Here H_* is a fixed k-qubit Clifford, R_* a fixed core Pauli, u,v,ell
are affine Boolean functions, and the scalar is a central Clifford
phase. The Pauli convention is ordered X then Z. To establish this
form, remove the affine core Pauli labels using controlled X and
controlled Z Clifford gates; the remaining operation is a diagonal
Clifford on the center. Its exponent is linear plus twice quadratic
modulo four. A Hermitian-Pauli convention adds only the explicit factor
$`i^{u(z)\cdot v(z)}`$. In either convention the scalar digits have
degree at most four. All fixed phases in (12) are retained.

The exact native scalar alphabet is finite. The correction (11) and
the conjugated Hermitian Pauli H are native Cliffords. Their core blocks
have eighth-root common phases. For example, compare a nonzero block
matrix element with that of a fixed projective core Clifford: its ratio
belongs to $`\mathbb Z[i,1/\sqrt2]`$ and has modulus one, so the
retained unit-circle lemma makes it an eighth root. A common arbitrary
scalar attached to the original V has already been separated in the
theorem statement and does not enter these conjugated pair factors.

Equations (9) and (12) show that each exact pair fiber F_j(z) is
determined by at most $`q_k=2k+4`$ Boolean features, each of degree
at most four: the two core Pauli labels, scalar digits, and the sign
of $`\mathcal R`$.

## 4. Arbitrarily many commuting pairs use a finite matrix alphabet

Every F_j(z) lies in one fixed finite alphabet $`\mathcal A_k`$
depending only on k. To bound its size, the number of core Cliffords
including eighth-root phases is at most
$`2^{2k^2+3k+3}`$: use the Pauli factor and the order of the binary
symplectic group. There are $`2^{2k+1}`$ signed Hermitian core Paulis
and two choices in (9). The generous bound

```math
|\mathcal A_k|\le N_k=2^{2k^2+5k+10}               \qquad(13)
```

therefore includes every possible pair fiber. Include only the valid
unitary matrices from these formulas; every such attained matrix has
order dividing eight by (7).

For each type A in this alphabet and each pair j, the selector

```math
\eta_{j,A}(z)=\mathbf1[F_j(z)=A]
```

is a Boolean function of that pair's at-most-q_k features. Its degree
is at most $`4q_k`$, since an arbitrary Boolean function of q_k bits
has an algebraic normal form of degree at most q_k. Define
$`n_A(z)=\sum_j\eta_{j,A}(z)`$ as an integer count. Its three digits
modulo eight have Boolean degree at most $`16q_k`$. These three binary
digits use elementary symmetric polynomials of orders one, two and four
in the summand bits; the largest carry degree is four.

On every actual central input, all matrices with nonzero counts commute,
because the original F_j commute. Thus in any one fixed ordering of the
alphabet,

```math
\prod_jF_j(z)=\prod_{A\in\mathcal A_k}A^{n_A(z)\bmod8}. \qquad(14)
```

The alphabet is not asserted to be pairwise commuting on arbitrary
unattained assignments. Only the simultaneous actual fiber matrices
need commute. Equation (14) is pointwise exact and uses at most
$`3N_k`$ features of degree at most $`16q_k`$, with no dependence
on the number of pairs.

## 5. The residual and silent factors have bounded features too

After the silent A factor and all pairs are peeled, a singleton B axis
has $`N_Pb=0`$ for every P in M. Each remaining A axis is active for
some P. Independence of the A axes therefore forces each singleton
to commute every remaining A axis individually. The source
$`U=E_{B,\mathrm{single}}E_A`$ is a commuting C3 phase polynomial
on an isotropic Pauli space L.

For each preserved input P, its image under U is a Clifford whose
symplectic action fixes $`L^\perp`$ pointwise. Its displacement image
lies in L. The native active C axes span these displacement images,
so every globally active C axis lies in L. Peel globally silent C
axes to the output as a native phase D_C commuting M. The residual is

```math
V_0=K_C^\dagger W_0K_A,\qquad
W_0=E_{C,\mathrm{active}}^\dagger U,                \qquad(15)
```

where W_0 is a commuting C3 phase on L and V_0 commutes M. The complete
factorization, with its actual order retained, is

```math
V=D_CV_0\left(\prod_jF_j\right)D_A.               \qquad(16)
```

To control the endpoint Cliffords in (15), set
$`A_0=K_A^\dagger L^\perp K_A`$. V_0 maps every A_0 Pauli to a
Pauli literally and fixes M pointwise. Its action on $`A_0+M`$ is a
consistent signed symplectic isometry, since it is the restriction of
actual unitary conjugation. Extend the binary isometry to a full
symplectic automorphism and extend its residual signs by a Pauli
character. This gives a Clifford C matching the exact action on A_0
and fixing M pointwise. Put

```math
J=K_CCK_A^\dagger,\qquad
V_1=C^\dagger V_0=K_A^\dagger J^\dagger W_0K_A.    \qquad(17)
```

J commutes every Pauli in $`L^\perp`$ literally. Hence J belongs to
the Pauli algebra of L and is a commuting-axis Clifford. V_1 is a
commuting C3 phase on $`K_A^\dagger LK_A`$. Since it also commutes
M, its Pauli support lies in the isotropic intersection

```math
\mathcal T=(K_A^\dagger LK_A)\cap M^\perp.          \qquad(18)
```

Pauli-basis uniqueness proves this support statement. The phase factors
through the eigenvalues of $`\mathcal T`$; restricting to a fixed
linear section gives a weighted cubic phase in those variables.
In the fixed central/core coordinates, the core projections of
$`\mathcal T`$ commute. One fixed core Clifford diagonalizes them.
Their eigenvalue bits are core Z bits plus central affine parities.
Thus V_1 has a fixed core-Clifford conjugate of a weighted cubic phase
polynomial in central and core bits. There is no center-dependent
choice of a diagonalizing Clifford.

For a weighted cubic exponent modulo eight, each binary digit has
Boolean degree at most four. This follows by binary carrying: weight-one
terms are linear, weight-two terms have degree at most two, and weight-four
terms degree at most three. A carry into the four's digit has total
weight at most four. Evaluating the three digits at each of the
$`2^k`$ fixed core strings therefore determines V_1(z) with
$`3\,2^k`$ degree-at-most-four features.

Each silent factor D_A,D_C is itself a commuting native phase belonging
to the algebra of $`M^\perp`$. Diagonalizing its commuting core
projections by one fixed core Clifford gives the same feature count.
Finally, C fixes M, so its blocks have form (12). At most $`2k+3`$
further features of degree at most four determine them. Altogether
the two silent factors and V_0 require at most

```math
B_k=9\,2^k+2k+3                                \qquad(19)
```

such features. Multiplying these fixed-size matrices in the order (16)
with the pair aggregate (14) proves the finite-feature theorem.

## 6. Boolean phase extraction and scope

Include the at-most-k core input bits as features when selecting a
matrix entry. If that entry is, on the specified target sector, a fixed
scalar times $`(-1)^{g}`$, equal feature vectors give the same sign.
The sign is consequently a Boolean function of the features on their
attainable assignments. Extend it arbitrarily elsewhere. The degree of
the composed function is at most the sum of the feature-degree bounds:

```math
\deg g\le4B_k+3N_k(16q_k)+k=D_k.                  \qquad(20)
```

No intermediate pair factor was asserted Clifford. Its explicit active
Pauli-sector Clifford correction, finite exact matrix alphabet, order
eight, and mutual commutation supply the joint bound. No count of
physical dirty wires or number of pairs appears. Every inverse and
native scalar in the original factorization remains literal; the proof
does not supply an uncharged physical coordinate transformation or an
additional synthesis circuit.

Equation (1) remains essential. A circuit specified only on one
initialized clean sector need not preserve the required subgroup on
the unused sector. Applying this theorem to such a circuit requires a
separate exact full-space reduction establishing (1).

## 7. One Pauli interior clean image suffices

Consider an actual native three-band circuit with one clean qubit c,
arbitrary dirty wires, and complete clean and dirty return. Its occupied
action is a literal or projective Boolean sign G. In the factorization
(4), define the **two interior clean images**

```math
J=E_AK_AZ_cK_A^\dagger E_A^\dagger,\qquad
S=E_CK_CZ_cK_C^\dagger E_C^\dagger=E_BJE_B^\dagger.
                                                               \qquad(21)
```

If J or S is a signed Pauli, then

```math
\deg_{\mathbb F_2}g\le2^{26},\qquad
\deg_{\mathbb F_2}f\le2^{26}-1
                                                               \qquad(22)
```

for a sign $`G=(-1)^{g(x)}`$ or a literal Boolean XOR query f,
respectively. The condition concerns an interior image in (21), not
the input or final clean stabilizer. If both interior images are Pauli,
the stronger [Pauli-boundary bound](PAULI_BOUNDARY_THREE_LAYER_RIGIDITY.md)
already applies. It remains to treat S Pauli and J non-Pauli; the other
case follows by reversing the actual circuit.

### 7.1. A paid two-band extension and one projected band

Choose an actual Clifford K_S with $`K_SZ_cK_S^\dagger=S`$ and set

```math
U_A=E_AK_A,\qquad U_B=E_B^\dagger K_S,\qquad
M_0=U_B^\dagger U_A=K_S^\dagger E_BE_AK_A.
```

These one-band encoders have the same non-Pauli clean image J. The
[two-band clean-removal construction](TWO_LAYER_DIAGONAL_RIGIDITY.md#2-removing-one-clean-initialization-in-the-non-pauli-branch)
therefore supplies actual Cliffords D_A,D_B, each fixing every clean-zero
vector literally, such that

```math
D_B^\dagger M_0D_A=U\otimes I_c                       \qquad(23)
```

on the whole physical space. To recall the construction, choose a
clean-active Pauli H in the common intrinsic active span of the two
encoders. H commutes both complete native bands and anticommutes with J.
Its two Pauli preimages anticommute with Z_c. Controlled-Pauli Cliffords
D_A,D_B align those preimages with X_c while fixing the clean-zero
sector. The dressed transition then commutes both X_c and Z_c, proving
(23) with its literal occupied phase. Thus $`U\otimes I_c`$ retains
an actual two-native-band representation. No extension of an arbitrary
occupied Clifford was used.

Write $`F=K_C^\dagger E_C^\dagger K_S`$. Both F and D_B preserve
Z_c, and

```math
VD_A=FD_B(U\otimes I_c).
```

Since S is Pauli, native independence forces every C axis to commute
the Pauli $`K_CZ_cK_C^\dagger`$, which is then equal to S. Consequently
the occupied restriction of FD_B consists of a projected single native
band and logical Cliffords. The projected band has commuting signed
logical axes with at most one binary relation: the kernel of the
physical-to-logical quotient is the one-dimensional stabilizer span.

Retain every identity-axis scalar and every inverse orientation. Choose
a maximal independent sublist of the nonidentity projected axes. At
most one axis remains outside this sublist. The projected band is
therefore an independent logical native band times at most one further
Pauli T or T-dagger phase. This includes a possible duplicate axis;
no cancellation or phase convention is needed. The factors commute,
so the extra phase can be moved to the outer end of the band, and an
adjacent logical Clifford conjugates its axis to another Pauli.

Extend these logical operations by identity on c, now regarded as an
arbitrary dirty wire. Equation (23) gives a literal full-space identity

```math
G\otimes I_c=\gamma T_P^\epsilon H_0,\qquad
\epsilon\in\{1,-1\},                              \qquad(24)
```

where H_0 has at most three native bands and gamma is the retained fixed
scalar. If there is no extra phase, omit $`T_P^\epsilon`$. The new
third band is independent on the logical wires and hence independent
after tensoring with identity on c. Equation (24) is an extension of
the entire occupied operator, including arbitrary dirty inputs and
external references. It need not agree with the original V on its
unused clean sector.

The two dressings in (23) add at most $`10(Q-1)+6`$ elementary Cliffords
to the forward construction. The same bound applies separately to its
actual inverse construction. The fixed code
coordinates and logical Clifford embeddings can be synthesized with
$`O(Q^2)`$ H/S/CNOT gates, with their inverses charged separately.
There is no added width or initialized workspace. The argument does not
call (24) a three-layer synthesis of G: its outside Pauli phase is
explicitly retained for the following finite-feature estimate.

### 7.2. Full-space partial diagonality and degree

Because G is diagonal, (24) implies that H_0 commutes literally with
the computational-Z subgroup

```math
M=\{Z(v):\langle P,Z(v)\rangle=0\}.
```

This subgroup has codimension at most one on all Q physical wires.
Apply Sections 1--6 with k at most one. The outside Pauli phase has a
fixed core axis and an affine central sign, so it adds at most one
degree-one feature. Thus

```math
\deg g\le D_1+1=37748830\lt2^{26},                 \qquad(25)
```

which gives the convenient bound in (22). The sign is independent of
the dirty coordinates, but the full operator argument allows all of
them and never initializes them. A fixed affine change of computational
coordinates preserves the stated algebraic-degree upper bound.

For a Boolean XOR query, conjugate the output by Hadamards. The clean
images in (21) are unchanged because that endpoint Clifford acts on a
logical wire and commutes Z_c. The resulting sign is
$`(-1)^{z f(x)}`$, whose degree is $`1+\deg f`$ for nonzero f.
This proves the query bound; the zero function is immediate. Reversing
the actual native word exchanges the two interior images and gives
the J-Pauli case, with its actual inverse gates and scalar retained.

The retained [R74 approximation-to-exactness consequence](CLEAN_CODE_CLIFFORD_RIGIDITY.md#63-boolean-approximation-reduces-to-the-exact-occupied-action)
gives the same obstruction below occupied operator error
$`2^{-530690}`$, also with a freely optimized common phase. It first
forces exact occupied Boolean action for the supplied depth-three
circuit; the interior-Pauli hypothesis still concerns that actual
circuit. The theorem leaves open the branch in which both interior
clean images are non-Pauli, including its zero-pair case. It does not
change a complete-frame synthesis resource bound.

The [exact regression fixtures](../../tests/test_partial_diagonal_three_layer_features.py)
retain a pair correction whose full Clifford extension is not Hermitian,
and commuting physical pair factors whose matrix alphabet contains
noncommuting core types. They check the native scalars, order-eight
count reduction and actual inverses. The width-independent result
follows from the proof above.


## 8. Both non-Pauli images of active rank one can be repacked

There is also a closed subcase in which neither interior image in (21)
is Pauli. Suppose each has exactly one clean-active native endpoint
axis. Equivalently, their intrinsic displacement ranks are both one.
Then the actual circuit admits a different three-band representation
whose two interior clean images are Pauli. Consequently the stronger
[Pauli-boundary theorem](PAULI_BOUNDARY_THREE_LAYER_RIGIDITY.md) gives

```math
\deg_{\mathbb F_2}g\le2048,\qquad
\deg_{\mathbb F_2}f\le2047.                         \qquad(26)
```

This is a change of the actual native representation, not an inference
that the supplied non-Pauli codes were Pauli. Write

```math
\begin{gathered}
P_A=K_AZ_cK_A^\dagger,\qquad
P_C=K_CZ_cK_C^\dagger,\\
E_A=E_{A,a}E_{A,s},\qquad
E_C=E_{C,c}E_{C,s},\\
J=E_{A,a}P_AE_{A,a}^\dagger,\qquad
S=E_{C,c}P_CE_{C,c}^\dagger.
\end{gathered}                                    \qquad(27)
```

The factors with subscripts a and c are the single active T or
T-dagger phases, with nonzero binary labels a and c. The s factors
are the corresponding clean-silent native subbands. All signed axes,
orientations and literal scalar phases are retained.

Native matching says J permutes the independent B axes up to signs.
Its binary action is the transvection
$`v\mapsto v+\langle a,v\rangle a`$. A swapped pair would have
labels b and $`b+a`$ with $`\langle a,b\rangle=1`$, so those
two B axes would anticommute. This contradicts a native band's
commutation. Every B axis is therefore a singleton and commutes a.
Since E_B commutes each of its axes, the equality
$`S=E_BJE_B^\dagger`$ shows that S fixes their labels too.
Thus every B axis also commutes c.

Let $`\mathcal B_-`$ be the B axes negated by J and let E_{B,-}
be their actual native subband. Because every B axis commutes a,
its sign under J is its sign under P_A. Thus $`\mathcal B_-`$
is exactly the subset anticommuting with P_A. Put
$`E_B=E_{B,-}E_{B,+}`$. The latter factor commutes P_A and J.
The signed native defect and the two endpoint transvections give

```math
\sum_{b\in\mathcal B_-}b\langle b,\cdot\rangle
 =a\langle a,\cdot\rangle+c\langle c,\cdot\rangle .
                                                        \qquad(28)
```

There is no product displacement term in (28), because all B axes
commute a. Each negative B sign contributes one S or S-dagger
transvection; its orientation does not change the binary formula.

Native independence makes the rank on the left exactly
$`|\mathcal B_-|`$. If a=c, the subset is empty. If a and c are
distinct, its size is two and its span is $`\langle a,c\rangle`$.
In that basis, (28) says that the two B column vectors form a binary
matrix R with $`RR^\mathsf T=I_2`$. The only such matrices are
the identity and the swap: a length-two binary vector has odd norm
exactly when it is a coordinate vector. Therefore

```math
\mathcal B_-=\varnothing\quad(a=c),\qquad
\mathcal B_-=\{a,c\}\quad(a\ne c).                  \qquad(29)
```

In the second case a and c commute, since they are B axes. Define
the literal operation

```math
F_0=E_{C,c}^\dagger E_{B,-}E_{A,a}.                \qquad(30)
```

For a=c, (30) combines two odd T exponents on one label. For
distinct a,c, it combines two odd exponents on each of two commuting
labels. Hence F_0 is an actual Clifford, including its scalar.
If signed representatives differ, first use the exact identity
$`T_{-P}=\omega T_P^\dagger`$; the resulting powers of omega
remain in F_0. No projective cancellation is used.
The clean-image identity in (27) and the commutation of E_{B,+}
with J give

```math
F_0P_AF_0^\dagger=P_C.                            \qquad(31)
```

The active factors commute with one another and with E_{B,+}.
Keeping the silent subbands in their original order gives the
full-space identity

```math
V=K_C^\dagger E_{C,s}^\dagger
       F_0E_{B,+}E_{A,s}K_A.                      \qquad(32)
```

Set

```math
K_C'=F_0^\dagger K_C,\qquad
E_{C,s}'=F_0^\dagger E_{C,s}F_0.
```

Then (32) becomes
$`V=(K_C')^\dagger(E_{C,s}')^\dagger E_{B,+}E_{A,s}K_A`$.
Each of its three bands preserves P_A; both endpoint Cliffords
send Z_c to P_A. Conjugating the C silent band by an actual Clifford
preserves physical axis independence. Thus these are actual native
bands with Pauli clean images at every boundary. The repacking uses
no additional T band, wire or initialized workspace. Its adjacent
Clifford products and conjugations are actual operations, whose
elementary Clifford synthesis costs must be charged whenever a
physical resource ledger is requested.

Equation (32) is literal on both clean sectors and on arbitrary dirty
inputs, including external references. Taking its adjoint gives the
actual inverse with F_0's scalar conjugated and every T orientation
reversed; it is not a separately chosen occupied inverse. Applying
the Pauli-boundary theorem to this representation proves (26).
The same [R74 approximation consequence](CLEAN_CODE_CLIFFORD_RIGIDITY.md#63-boolean-approximation-reduces-to-the-exact-occupied-action)
applies below occupied error $`2^{-530690}`$.
The general branch with two non-Pauli interior images remains open;
the argument here uses both active ranks being one.
