# Scalar-field automorphisms force a four-address radical relation

[Field query](FIELD_SHEAR_INTERTWINER.md) ·
[Complete copy grading](DIRTY_COPY_GRADING.md) ·
[Clifford output duals](CLIFFORD_DUAL_COPY_GRADING.md)

An associative radical algebra offers a natural way to construct a trace-graded
Clifford family: its unit group acts regularly by binary affine permutations,
so every nonidentity word has zero trace. For a scalar-field shear query,
however, the algebra-automorphism condition itself forces a four-address
identity word. This holds for every address subspace, arbitrary numbers of
identical field banks, enlarged fields and arbitrary query-fixed coordinates.
The resulting error floor is $`1/2`$ for a direct copy, or $`1/4`$ when the
family is a transported Clifford output dual of a proposed copy.

The theorem concerns this associative-algebra construction. It does not
exclude arbitrary Clifford duals, arbitrary regular affine groups, non-scalar
phase dressings, or the field query itself. In particular, changing the address
embedding still defines a valid field query; it does not reopen this algebra
model.

## 1. The proposed regular-affine construction

Let A be a finite-dimensional associative nilpotent algebra over the binary
field, without requiring a multiplicative identity. Its unitization contains
the group $`1+A`$. On the underlying affine space of A, define

```math
L_a(y)=a+y+ay,\qquad L_aL_b=L_{a+b+ab}.                 \qquad(1)
```

These maps are binary affine permutations and hence Clifford operations.
If $`L_a(y)=y`$, then $`a(1+y)=0`$. Nilpotence makes $`1+y`$
invertible, so a is zero. Thus every nonidentity group element has no fixed
computational basis vector and its literal permutation matrix has zero trace.
This would supply the complete trace-grading condition if the desired orbit
generators had independent occurrence parities.

Assume the underlying vector space decomposes as

```math
A=U\oplus W\oplus H,\qquad U=W=F^r,\qquad
F=\mathbb F_{2^k},\qquad r\ge1.                       \qquad(2)
```

Choose any p-dimensional binary subspace S of F. Suppose every literal map

```math
Q_\lambda(u,w,h)=(u,w+\lambda u,h),\qquad\lambda\in S, \qquad(3)
```

is an algebra automorphism. Coordinates in H are arbitrary and query-fixed;
no prepared value is assumed. Different field banks carry the same scalar
$`\lambda`$. Let $`a^2=0`$, and put

```math
a_\lambda=Q_\lambda(a),\qquad g_\lambda=1+a_\lambda,
\qquad J_\lambda=L_{a_\lambda}.                       \qquad(4)
```

Every generator is an involution. Moreover an algebra automorphism conjugates
$`L_a`$ to $`L_{Q_\lambda(a)}`$, so (4) is precisely the proposed query orbit.

## 2. The image of the shear is square-zero

The following structural lemma needs only a binary-bilinear multiplication
preserved by (3). Associativity and nilpotence are not used until the group
consequence.

**Lemma.** If $`p\ge2`$, then $`WW=0`$.

Choose a nonzero $`\lambda_0\in S`$, write
$`S'=S/\lambda_0`$, and let K be the subfield of F generated by S'. Set

```math
N=Q_{\lambda_0}-I,\qquad N_s=Q_{\lambda_0s}-I=sN
\quad(s\in S'),\qquad Z=\ker N=W\oplus H.            \qquad(5)
```

Here s multiplies vectors in W by their inherited field scalar. The map N
sends U onto W. If $`z\in Z`$, multiplicativity gives

```math
N(uz)=(Nu)z,\qquad N(zu)=z(Nu).                       \qquad(6)
```

Consequently $`WZ,ZW\subseteq W`$. Compare (6) with the same identity for
$`N_s=sN`$. For $`w\in W`$ and $`z\in Z`$,

```math
(sw)z=s(wz),\qquad z(sw)=s(zw).                       \qquad(7)
```

The scalars satisfying both identities are closed under addition and
multiplication and contain one. Their finite integral subring is a subfield,
so (7) holds for all of K. In particular the product $`W\times W\to W`$
is K-bilinear. Notice that its range cannot enter H.

Put $`C=U\oplus W`$ and let $`\pi:A\to C`$ be the projection along H.
It commutes with every query. The map
$`\mu(u,v)=\pi(uv)`$ on $`C\times C`$ is binary-bilinear and query-equivariant.
Average it over independent nonzero scalars on its two inputs:

```math
\nu(u,v)=\sum_{\alpha,\beta\in K^\times}
 (\alpha\beta)^{-1}\mu(\alpha u,\beta v).              \qquad(8)
```

All output scalings in (8) act on C, where they are defined on both U and W.
They commute with the query, so $`\nu`$ remains query-equivariant.
Reindexing $`\alpha`$ or $`\beta`$ proves that $`\nu`$ is K-bilinear.
For $`w,z\in W`$, every summand in (8) equals wz by (7). There are
$`(|K|-1)^2`$ summands, an odd number, hence

```math
\nu(w,z)=wz.                                         \qquad(9)
```

Equivariance under $`I+N`$ and $`I+sN`$, respectively, gives

```math
N\nu(u,v)=\nu(Nu,v)+\nu(u,Nv)+\nu(Nu,Nv),
```
```math
sN\nu(u,v)=s\nu(Nu,v)+s\nu(u,Nv)+s^2\nu(Nu,Nv).       \qquad(10)
```

Subtract s times the first identity from the second. Since $`p\ge2`$,
S' contains s outside the binary field, so $`s^2+s\ne0`$. Therefore
$`\nu(Nu,Nv)=0`$. Surjectivity of $`N:C\to W`$ and (9) prove $`WW=0`$.

The averaging is an algebraic proof device, not an operation in a quantum
circuit. It introduces no prepared register, random choice or computational
resource claim.

## 3. A literal four-address word and its error floor

Write $`w_\lambda=(Q_\lambda-I)a\in W`$. Since both a and
$`a+w_\lambda`$ square to zero, the lemma implies

```math
aw_\lambda=w_\lambda a,\qquad
w_\lambda w_\mu=0.                                   \qquad(11)
```

The differences are additive in the address. Thus the involutions in (4)
commute and satisfy

```math
g_\lambda g_\mu=g_0g_{\lambda+\mu}.                  \qquad(12)
```

For every base v and two independent address directions s,t, (12) gives

```math
g_vg_{v+s}g_{v+t}g_{v+s+t}=1.                         \qquad(13)
```

All four addresses are distinct. Equation (13) holds literally in the regular
affine representation, with phase one on every computational column. If the
seed has zero U component the orbit is constant and already has a two-address
identity word.

A complete copy with per-generator operator error at most $`\epsilon`$
would send the left side of (13) within $`4\epsilon`$ of its product tensored
with four distinct physical X probes. The original product is the identity;
the target product is a nontrivial Pauli and has distance two from it. Hence

```math
\epsilon\ge\frac12.                                 \qquad(14)
```

For a transported Clifford output-dual family of a proposed original copy,
the retained dual-conjugation estimate gives per-generator signed-conjugation
error at most $`2\eta`$. Choose the plane through the selected output index;
its four desired signs have product minus one. Equation (13) then yields

```math
\eta\ge\frac14.                                      \qquad(15)
```

Individual scalar phase factors change (13) only to a scalar unitary, so the
same contradictions and floors hold. A common disjoint query-fixed involution
and a fixed simultaneous conjugation also preserve the relation. General
non-scalar Pauli or Clifford dressings are outside the assertion unless their
full word is separately evaluated.

## 4. Changed addresses and moving helper targets

The proof did not assume canonical powers, a multiplicative collision in S,
or that the ratio field K equals all of F. In particular an injective map
$`\mathrm{Sym}^2(S)\to F`$ does not rescue this construction. The closure
and averaging in (6)--(10) supply the obstruction even when that injectivity
holds. Enlarging F or adding identical field banks also does not help.

For the explicit exponent choice $`d_i=i+(2p-1)i^2`$ and
$`S=\mathrm{span}\{t^{d_i}:0\le i\lt p\}`$, there is an additional simple
check. When $`p\ge3`$, $`d_1=2p`$ and $`d_2=8p-2`$ have greatest common
divisor two. The ratio field contains their powers of t and their inverses,
hence contains $`t^2`$ by a Bezout identity. Squaring is an automorphism of a
finite binary field, so it contains t and therefore all of F. This observation
is consistent with the lemma but is not needed for it.

The enlarged-field character identity with a moving residual helper query
remains a valid abstract target. Its native decoder is still unpaid. This
screen retires only the associative radical-algebra realization under (2)--(3);
a different Clifford-dual construction or a jointly synthesized native decoder
remains open.

## 5. Exact fixtures and scope

The [focused fixture](../../tests/test_scalar_field_radical_copy.py) computes the complete binary-linear space of
query-equivariant bilinear multiplications in small one-bank, multiple-bank
and fixed-helper cases and verifies that every such multiplication vanishes
on $`W\times W`$. It also checks a one-address counterexample, showing why
$`p\ge2`$ is needed, and the literal four-address word in a regular affine
representation of a strictly upper-triangular matrix algebra.

These exact cases test the algebraic steps and complete permutation action;
they do not replace the all-size proof or construct a shallow copy. No new
unrestricted native-depth lower bound or improved general frame upper bound
follows from this restricted-family screen.
