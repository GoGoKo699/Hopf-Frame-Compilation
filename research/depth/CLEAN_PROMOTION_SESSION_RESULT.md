## 2. The mathematical uncertainty that changed

The target is still

```math
I_p|x,Y,w\rangle=|x,Y\mathbin\oplus e_x,w\rangle,
\qquad m=2^p.
```

For every fixed requested tolerance tau, the desired zero-clean native
word has $`T,b_{\rm dirty}=O_\tau(m)`$,
$`G=O_\tau(m^2)`$ and $`D_T=o(\log(p+2))`$. Its literal
full-operator comparison includes arbitrary address, output, borrowed
work and references, plus the actual inverse and every return error.

The [clean-removal theorem](DIRTY_PROMOTION_OF_CLEAN_QUERIES.md)
shows that zero clean work need not be imposed on the initial mechanism
discovery. A complete source query with a fixed number of clean helpers
can be localized to its initialized sector and then extended over all
sectors using borrowed work. Neither controlling that source nor
specifying its off-code action is necessary. Approximate clean return,
including leakage, is enough with an explicit error amplification.

This is stronger than merely replacing the clean input by an unknown
dirty state. The actual selective commutators cancel arbitrary off-code
dynamics, and the translated sector cover makes the final query correct
on the entire borrowed space. A prepared state or one successful output
column does not meet the source premise.

## 3. Strongest positive result and the complete recurrence

Let V implement a q-output complete XOR query Q_f using k clean
source bits, with clean-isometry error epsilon on all address, output
and private dirty inputs. Put $`M=2^k`$. The proof home supplies
the literal selective word, its cyclic Gray cover, and common
forward/actual-inverse resource bounds

```math
\begin{aligned}
T_{\rm prom}&\le M(4T_V+4T_C+24q+4),\\
G_{\rm prom}&\le M(4G_V+4G_C+40q+16)+2q,\\
D_T(\mathrm{prom})&\le M(4d_V+4d_C+16),\\
b_{\rm prom}&\le k+q+1+b_V+b_C.
\end{aligned}
```

C is the retained exact returned k-bit zero-test. Every helper is
borrowed. A block-polar comparison, used only in the proof, yields
full-operator error at most $`8M\epsilon`$. If the actual approximate
V preserves its clean sector exactly, the translated words have
disjoint sector supports and the stronger error bound is
$`2\epsilon`$.

For indicators $`q=m`$, a uniformly bounded clean count preserves all
three requested count/width orders and sublogarithmic depth. The
complete R98 transfer is paid in the proof home, using at most four
indicator occurrences per completed word query and the retained
constant long-query count. The original literal frame widths and R96
fallbacks remain intact. For arbitrary word queries with q much larger
than the row count, the additional q terms need not fit the original
budget; that stronger transfer is not asserted.

A weaker source asks only for a native U with one signal a and

```math
\|J^\dagger UJ-Q_f/2\|\le\varepsilon,
\qquad J|\psi\rangle=|0\rangle_a|\psi\rangle.
```

For $`\varepsilon\le1/8`$, the retained normalization-two amplification has full clean-isometry
error at most $`6\varepsilon+4\varepsilon^2`$, including rejected
amplitude. Its fixed literal source phase cancels in the subsequent
actual-adjoint conjugations. After promotion the complete bounds are

```math
\begin{aligned}
T&\le24T_U+48q+8,\\
G&\le24G_U+82q+104,\\
D_T&\le24d_U+32,\qquad b\le q+b_U+2,\\
\|\widetilde Q_f-Q_f\otimes I\|
&\le16(6\varepsilon+4\varepsilon^2).
\end{aligned}
```

A block at amplitude $`1/\sqrt2`$ has a paid phase-S recovery with
the same resource majorants and error
$`16(3\sqrt2\varepsilon+2\varepsilon^2)`$. The positive-S
cost of its actual inverse is counted separately. These two
normalizations are alternatives, not simultaneous requirements.

The first unpaid operation is now explicit: emit such a complete native
block with linear T-count and dirty width, quadratic Clifford count,
and sublogarithmic T-depth. A formal unitary dilation or a short accepted
path sum does not supply that operation.

## 4. What the candidate limits do and do not establish

The generic sector cover has exponential source-call overhead in k.
This is not just an inefficient choice of Gray order: any source-oblivious
wrapper that promotes both a sector-controlled identity and a
sector-controlled X to full-space identity and X, at error below one,
needs at least $`2^k`$ source calls. The proof uses rank telescoping.
It is a black-box call bound; it does not exclude a source-dependent
joint native synthesis or establish a T-depth lower bound.

The [joint Fourier-word audit](JOINT_FOURIER_WORD_LIMITS.md) tests a
non-Gaussian repeated word. For a Boolean work phase f of degree at most
d, the word $`(H^{\otimes r}D_f)^4`$ either equals a specified sign
exactly or has operator distance at least $`2^{2-d}`$ from that sign.
Exact scalar closure determines the sign by
$`f(1^r)+f(0^r)`$. Thus cubic work phases retain a one-half gap;
approximation does not rescue this particular repeated architecture.
Distinct phase words and address motion remain outside its hypotheses.

One Boolean diagonal between Clifford endpoints cannot supply a full
half-amplitude unitary block below error $`1/\sqrt2-1/2`$.
One physical T/T-dagger layer has the smaller positive floor
$`1/2-\sin(\pi/8)`$. The singular spectrum alone leaves
amplitude $`1/\sqrt2`$ available. Pursuing that alternative exposes
more structure: sufficiently accurate one-physical-band compression at
this amplitude is exactly flat. Its input/output clean Paulis
anticommute, and no original native axis is active on both. The actual
projected axes give a zero-clean logical circuit of at most two T
layers, including the cases with one projection relation. The retained
two-layer rigidity theorem therefore gives the full block-error floor
$`\sin(\pi/8)/4`$ for complete indicators with $`p\ge3`$.
This excludes both proposed normalizations for a one-physical-band
source. It leaves two or more noncommuting source bands open, and is
not an unrestricted growing-depth lower bound.

The [indicator nullity calculation](INDICATOR_PAULI_NULLITY.md)
uses the established unitary stabilizer-nullity method and proves
$`T\ge m-1`$ at error below $`1/\sqrt2`$, with unrestricted
native mixing and arbitrary dirty width. Together with the retained
linear-count implementation it fixes the standalone count order.
An explicit one-layer word saturates the earlier per-bank incidence
requirement while failing the indicator by operator distance two.
Incidence alone therefore misses the required joint row discrimination.
Neither component settles growing depth.

## 5. The focused lower scout

PR #196 already closed growing total displacement in the actual
no-middle-pair reduction. The present scout keeps the original
physical pairs and the literal global Boolean condition.

The [occupied-center theorem](OCCUPIED_BOOLEAN_PAIR_CENTER.md)
assumes both endpoint clean-active lists are nonempty, as they are
whenever original middle pairs occur. If a physical Pauli commutes the
initial clean Pauli and the first two actual bands, occupied Booleanity
forces it to commute the final clean Pauli and the entire final band.
The time-reversed statement also holds. This is an actual full-space
Pauli transport deduced from the occupied condition, not an assumption
that the unused sector is Boolean.

For m original physical pairs, a genuine common isotropic center R
then leaves transition quotient dimension at most $`3m+2`$ and
clean-preserving residual quotient dimension at most $`3m+1`$.
All three surviving logical silent lists remain independent for
physical reasons. At fixed m, the central dependence uses only finitely
many features of degree at most four, uniformly in the endpoint ranks.

The first unsupported inference would be to substitute this noncentral
dimension into the finite-insertion degree theorem. Its hypothesis
bounds total Pauli support; bounded central features do not do that.
A quadratic-controlled Pauli already has one noncentral qubit and one
feature while inducing growing Clifford displacement. Restricting
features can also create relations among the retained silent lists.
Consequently fixed positive physical-pair count, growing pair count,
and higher-layer asymptotics remain open. No growing-degree Boolean
counterfamily is claimed.
